### Course Overview  
The course titled **Circuit Theory and Devices** (Course Code: ECE215) is a 4-credit core course offered to second-year undergraduate students in the Electronics and Communication Engineering (ECE) department. This course aims to provide students with a comprehensive understanding of circuit theory, device modeling, and their applications. The primary objectives include developing problem-solving skills related to transient, sinusoidal, and frequency-domain circuit analysis techniques.

---

### Course Prerequisites  
The prerequisite for this course is **Circuits I** (ECE105). Students are expected to have a foundational knowledge of basic circuit concepts, including Kirchhoff's laws, resistor networks, and DC circuit analysis. No specific desirable prerequisites are mentioned, but proficiency in algebra and calculus is assumed.

---

### Course Outcomes  
By the end of this course, students will be able to:  
- **CO1:** Perform AC power analysis for single-phase, polyphase, and magnetically coupled circuits.  
- **CO2:** Apply circuit analysis techniques such as mesh, nodal, superposition, source transformation, and Thevenin/Norton equivalents.  
- **CO3:** Use Laplace transforms and Fourier series to analyze simple circuits and two-port networks.  
- **CO4:** Design, simulate, fabricate, and test simple circuits using industry-standard tools like LTSpice and PCB design software.

### Assessment Plan  
The course assessment is designed to evaluate both theoretical understanding and practical application skills. The breakdown is as follows:  
- **Mid-Semester Examination (25%)**: Covers the first half of the course material, including circuit analysis techniques and basic component modeling.  
- **End-Semester Examination (35%)**: Focuses on advanced topics such as frequency response, two-port networks, and filter design.  
- **Lab Assignments (20%)**: Includes hands-on experiments with LTSpice, PCB design tools, and circuit testing.  
- **Homework Assignments (20%)**: Reinforces theoretical concepts through problem-solving exercises.  

Additional types of evaluation may be included if necessary.
