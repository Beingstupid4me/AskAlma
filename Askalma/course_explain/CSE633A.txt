**Course Overview**

The course CSE633A, titled "Social Robotics" is a 2-credit module offered to both undergraduate (UG) and postgraduate (PG) students within the Computer Science and Engineering (CSE) department. This course provides an in-depth exploration of social robotics, focusing on its distinctiveness from traditional robotics, its applications across various domains, and human-robot interaction techniques. The curriculum is designed to equip students with a comprehensive understanding of the field, emphasizing both theoretical knowledge and practical skills.

**Prerequisites**

The course does not require any mandatory prerequisites. However, it is recommended that students have a basic understanding of robotics principles, computer science fundamentals, and an interest in human-robot interaction. Desirable prerequisites include familiarity with programming concepts and exposure to real-world applications of technology.

**Learning Outcomes**

By the end of this course, students will be able to:

1. **CO1:** Analyze the main challenges and opportunities associated with social robots.
2. **CO2:** Assess the design and interaction abilities of a social robot.
3. **CO3:** Predict the potential consequences of human-robot interactions in diverse scenarios and propose solutions using Ethical, Legal, and Social Implications (ELS) rules.
4. **CO4:** Understand the ethical, legal, and societal implications of social robotics.

**Weekly Syllabus**

The course is structured into eight weekly sessions:

1. **Week 1:** Introduction to Social Robotics - Understanding its differences from traditional robotics and exploring its applications.
2. **Week 2:** Social Robots: Embodiment and Design - Focusing on how robots are designed to be understood by humans.
3. **Week 3:** Social Robots' Skill Set - Covering social emotional intelligence (SEI) and socio-cognitive skills necessary for interaction.
4. **Week 4:** Social Robots and Communication Skills - Exploring effective communication methods with humans.
5. **Week 5:** Case Studies on Human Responses to Social Robots - Analyzing case studies involving individuals with autism, intellectual disabilities, and the elderly.
6. **Week 6:** Ethical, Legal, and Social Implications of Social Robotics - Discussing implications through invited lectures.
7. **Week 7:** Research Readings - Assignments based on provided research papers.
8. **Week 8:** Mini-Project - Applying learned concepts to develop a social robot project.

**Assessment Strategy**

The course assessment is designed to evaluate both theoretical understanding and practical application:

- **Class Participation (10%)**: Active engagement in discussions and group activities.
- **Assignments (20%)**: Practical tasks completed during the course weeks.
- **Quiz (10%)**: A formative assessment focusing on key concepts.
- **Mini-Project (30%)**: An individual or group project demonstrating practical skills.
- **End-Semester Examination (30%)**: Comprehensive evaluation of all course topics.

**Learning Resources**

Students are provided with essential resources, including:

- **Textbook:** "Social Robotics: Design, Control, and Applications" by B.C. Arumugam et al., which serves as the primary reference.
- **Research Papers:** Additional materials will be provided throughout the course for deeper understanding.
- **Other Resources:** Access to supplementary materials and updates via the course platform.

This structured approach ensures a well-rounded learning experience, combining theoretical knowledge with hands-on practical skills.
