### Course Overview  
This course, **Introduction to Functional Analysis (MTH542)**, is designed for both MTech and PhD students. It serves as a foundational course, bridging advanced linear algebra with mathematical analysis, focusing on infinite-dimensional spaces and their applications. The primary objective is to equip students with essential knowledge and skills in functional analysis, emphasizing the development of mathematical abilities while limiting extensive real-world applications.

### Prerequisites  
Students are expected to have completed **Linear Algebra (MTH100 for UG)** and possess a basic understanding of **Calculus and Complex Variables (MTH203 for UG)**. These prerequisites ensure that students have a solid foundation in mathematical concepts necessary for the course.

### Learning Outcomes (COs)  
By the end of this course, students will be able to:  
- **CO1**: Understand and apply fundamental concepts of functional analysis, including topology, metric spaces, normed spaces, Hilbert spaces, and spectral theory.  
- **CO2**: Analyze mathematical problems using rigorous proof techniques and epsilon-delta definitions.  
- **CO3**: Construct and evaluate subsidiary results related to the course topics.  
- **CO4**: Apply theoretical knowledge to specific function spaces and real-world problems.

### Weekly Lecture Plan  

#### Week 1: Topology of the Real Line  
Students will be introduced to basic topological concepts, focusing on compactness and completeness in metric spaces. Assignments include homework and a quiz covering related short problems.

#### Weeks 2-5: Metric Space Preliminaries  
This section covers convergence issues, fixed point theorems, sequences, and series of functions. Students will engage with weekly assignments and quizzes to reinforce their understanding.

#### Weeks 6-9: Normed Spaces  
Students will explore bounded linear transformations, major theorems (Hahn-Banach, Open Mapping), and spectral theory. Assignments include homework, tutorials, and quizzes.

#### Weeks 10-13: Hilbert Spaces  
This part delves into Lp spaces, Schauder bases, and spectral theory in Hilbert spaces. Students will complete weekly assignments and quizzes to apply their knowledge.

#### Week 14-21: Advanced Topics  
The course concludes with advanced topics such as compact operators and applications of the Spectral theorem. Assignments include homework, tutorials, and quizzes throughout these weeks.

### Assessment Strategy  

- **Test**: 10% contribution, focusing on foundational concepts.  
- **Mid-Semester Exam**: 20% weightage, covering comprehensive material up to Week 9.  
- **Weekly Submission and Quiz**: 25% total, emphasizing consistent engagement with course topics.  
- **End-Semester Exam**: 45% weightage, assessing in-depth understanding of all course content.

### Learning Resources  

#### Textbooks  
1. **Rudin: Principles of Mathematical Analysis, McGraw-Hill**  
   - A comprehensive textbook covering foundational concepts in real analysis and topology.  

2. **Aliprantis & Birkenshaw: Principles of Real Analysis, Elsevier**  
   - Offers a detailed exploration of measure theory and integration principles.

3. **Simmons: Introduction to Topology and Modern Analysis, McGraw-Hill**  
   - Provides an accessible introduction to topology and functional analysis.

4. **Saxe: Beginning Functional Analysis, Springer**  
   - A user-friendly guide to the basics of functional analysis, ideal for self-study.
