### Course Overview
**Course Name:** Calculus on ℝⁿ and Applications  
**Course Code:** MTH544  
**Credits:** 4  
**Offered To:** Undergraduate/Graduate Students  

---

### Course Description
This course provides an introductory overview of smooth differential forms on smooth manifolds. It serves as a sequel to multivariable calculus, extending concepts to higher-dimensional vector spaces and manifolds. The primary focus is on understanding calculus in ℝⁿ, starting with n = 3, before generalizing to more complex scenarios. The course also integrates applied themes, concluding with three distinct applications: engineering via optimization on manifolds, mathematics via de Rham cohomology of manifolds, and physics via modeling Maxwell’s equations using differential forms.

Key topics include:
- Review of multivariable calculus concepts (e.g., partial derivatives, multiple integrals).
- Introduction to differential forms through integration, exterior derivatives, wedge products, and properties in ℝⁿ.
- Exploration of Riemannian metrics, Hodge star maps, and inner products of differential forms.
- Stokes’ theorem, smooth manifolds, vector fields, pull-backs, and push-forwards.
- Applications in engineering, mathematics, and physics.

---

### Prerequisites
**Mandatory:**
- MTH100 (Linear Algebra)
- MTH203 (Multivariable Calculus) or MTH240 (Real Analysis I)

**Desirable:**
- Familiarity with metric spaces, point-set topology, basic differential geometry, and linear optimization.

---

### Course Outcomes
By the end of this course, students will be able to:
1. Explain basic definitions and facts related to differential forms in ℝ³.
2. Prove essential properties and perform computations with differential forms in specified coordinates on basic model manifolds.
3. Define smooth manifolds, vector fields, and apply pullback/pushforward maps of forms and vector fields.
4. Apply concepts to solve problems in engineering (optimization), mathematics (cohomology), and physics (Maxwell’s equations).
