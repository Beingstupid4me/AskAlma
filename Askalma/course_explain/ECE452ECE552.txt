**Course Overview**

The course titled "Statistical Signal Processing" is designed for both undergraduate (UG) and postgraduate (PG) students. It carries a credit value of 4. The course delves into advanced techniques for statistical signal processing, detection, and parameter estimation. The curriculum is structured to provide a comprehensive understanding of the subject matter, starting with foundational topics in linear algebra and statistics before progressing into hypothesis testing, estimation techniques, and adaptive filtering approaches.

**Prerequisites**

To successfully engage with this course, students are expected to have completed the following prerequisites:

- **Mandatory Prerequisites:**
  - Signals & Systems

- **Desirable Prerequisites:**
  - Matlab Programming
  - Probability and Statistics
  - Linear Algebra

These prerequisites ensure that students possess a solid foundation in signal processing fundamentals and mathematical tools necessary for the course.

**Learning Outcomes**

Upon completion of this course, students will be able to:

1. **CO1:** Apply hypothesis testing techniques to solve signal detection problems.
2. **CO2:** Evaluate detector performance using metrics such as ROC curves, sensitivity, specificity, etc.
3. **CO3:** Choose appropriate estimation techniques (e.g., MLE, MAP, MMSE) based on the problem at hand.
4. **CO4:** Design and implement adaptive filters for stochastic signals.

### Weekly Lecture Plan  

| Week Number | Lecture Topic                     | Course Outcomes Met | Assignments/Labs/Tutorials                                                                 |
|-------------|-----------------------------------|---------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| 1           | Linear Algebra & Probability Review | CO1, CO2           | HW 1: Problems on sufficient statistics, probability concepts, Monte Carlo sampling.    |
| 2           | Sufficient Statistics & Significance Testing | CO1, CO2          | HW 1: Additional problems on sufficient statistics, probability, and Monte Carlo sampling.|
| 3           | Minimum Mean Square Error (MMSE), Cramér-Rao Lower Bound (CRLB), Best Unbiased Estimator (MVUE) | CO3               | - Problems on MMSE estimation and CRLB analysis.                                         |
| 4           | General MVUE, BLUE                 | CO3                | - Problems on general MVUE and Best Linear Unbiased Estimator (BLUE).                     |
| 5           | Maximum Likelihood (ML), MAP Estimation | CO3               | HW 2: Problems on ML estimation, MAP estimation.                                           |
| 6           | Neyman-Pearson Detector, Likelihood Ratio Test (LRT), ROC Curves | CO1, CO2          | HW 3: NP detector design and LRT analysis.                                                |
| 7           | Generalized Matched Filters, Estimator-Correlator | CO1, CO2         | HW 3: Problems on generalized matched filters and estimator-correlator techniques.        |
| 8           | Estimator-Correlator & GLRT          | CO1, CO2          | HW 4: Design of GLRT-based detectors.                                                      |
| 9           | Wiener Filter, Steepest Descent Algorithm | CO4               | - Problems on Wiener filter design and steepest descent algorithm implementation.            |
| 10          | Least Squares Adaptive Filters (LMS), Variants of Least Squares Adaptive Filters | CO4              | HW 5: Implementation of LMS and least squares-based adaptive filters.                     |
| 11          | Recursive Least Squares (RLS), Kalman Filter | CO4              | HW 6: Design of RLS-based adaptive filters and Kalman filter applications.                 |
| 12          | Robust Least Squares Estimation        | CO4              | - Problems on robust least squares estimation techniques.                                    |

Each week includes homework assignments that reinforce the concepts covered in lectures, with a focus on both theoretical understanding and practical application.

**Assessment Strategy**

The course employs a balanced assessment strategy to evaluate student learning:

- **Assignments:** Contribute 30% of the final grade, focusing on problem-solving and theoretical understanding.
- **Quiz:** Contributes 30%, testing immediate comprehension of topics covered in lectures.
- **Mid-Semester Exam:** Contributes 20%, assessing cumulative knowledge up to the midpoint of the course.
- **End-Semester Exam:** Contributes 20%, evaluating overall mastery of the subject.
