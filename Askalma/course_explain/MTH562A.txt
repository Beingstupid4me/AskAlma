**Course Overview**

The course code MTH562A is titled "Point Set Topology" and is worth 2 credits. It is designed for students pursuing both undergraduate (UG) and postgraduate (PG) studies, providing a foundational understanding of topology, which is essential for advanced studies in analysis, geometry, algebraic topology, and related fields.

**Course Description**

This course introduces the fundamental concepts of point set topology, focusing on the basic definitions and constructions used in topology. The curriculum covers topics such as topological spaces, connectedness, compactness, separation axioms, metrization theorems, and complete metric spaces. By the end of this course, students will be able to:

1. **CO1**: Determine whether a given set is open or closed.
2. **CO2**: Assess the continuity of mappings between topological spaces and construct such mappings.
3. **CO3**: Identify connected sets; determine compactness through finite products; and understand concepts like first and second countability, normality, and metrizability.
4. **CO4**: Differentiate between various topological properties such as normality, metrization, and completeness.

**Learning Outcomes**

The course is structured to ensure a comprehensive understanding of point set topology through weekly lectures that build upon each other. The topics covered include:

- Week 1: Introduction to topological spaces, basis for topology, standard topology on the real line, order topology, product topology, subspace topology, closed sets, limit points, Hausdorff axiom, metric topology.
- Week 4: Connectedness, path-connectedness, local connectedness, components, and applications of connectedness.
- Week 5: Compactness, Tube lemma, Lebesgue number lemma, limit point compactness, sequential compactness, locally compact spaces, one-point compactification.
- Week 6: Axioms of countability, regularity, normality, Urysohn's Lemma, and the Urysohn Metrization Theorem.
- Week 7: Complete metric spaces, Baire's theorem, and Ascoli's theorem.
