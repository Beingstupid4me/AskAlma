### Course Overview  
**Course Code:** ECE510  
**Course Name:** Digital Hardware Design (DHD)  
**Credits:** 4  
**Offered To:** Undergraduate/Postgraduate (UG/PG)  

This course provides an in-depth understanding of Field Programmable Gate Arrays (FPGAs) and their applications in digital system design. FPGAs are highlighted for their distributed and parallel architecture, which allows complete control over arithmetic operations and complex logic designs. The course combines theoretical concepts with practical labs to reinforce learning outcomes.

---

### Prerequisites  
**Mandatory Pre-requisite:** ECE270 Embedded Logic Design  
**Desirable Pre-requisite:** None specified  

Students are expected to have a foundational understanding of digital logic design, as provided in ECE270. This course builds upon that knowledge to explore advanced FPGA-based implementations.

---

### Learning Outcomes (COs)  
1. **CO1:** Students will be able to design and implement sequential and combinational logic on FPGA devices using hardware description languages like Verilog.  
2. **CO2:** Students will master the design and implementation of algorithms using hardware-level synthesis tools (HLS).  
3. **CO3:** Students will understand how to integrate FPGA switches, VGA interfaces, memory units, and embedded hardware components into complex systems.  
4. **CO4:** Students will be proficient in designing new hardware Intellectual Property (IP) blocks with appropriate AXI interfaces for scalable architectures.  
5. **CO5:** Students will gain hands-on experience in testing and troubleshooting FPGA-based designs using simulation tools.  
6. **CO6:** Students will develop the ability to analyze and optimize digital systems for performance, power consumption, and resource utilization.

### Assessment Strategy  

The course is assessed through a combination of quizzes, assignments, mid-term, and end-term examinations. Each component contributes to the final grade as follows:  
- **Quiz:** 20% (Evaluates understanding of weekly concepts)  
- **Assignment/Lab:** 20% (Assesses practical implementation skills)  
- **Mid-Semester Exam:** 25% (Comprehensive evaluation of first half of the course)  
- **End-Semester Exam:** 35% (In-depth assessment of overall learning outcomes)  

The end-semester examination carries a significant weight, reflecting its importance in evaluating students' mastery of FPGA-based digital system design.
