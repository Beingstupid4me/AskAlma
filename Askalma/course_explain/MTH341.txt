**Course Overview**

This course is coded as MTH341/541 and is titled "Complex Analysis." It offers 4 academic credits and is available for study during both undergraduate (UG) and postgraduate (PG) periods. The primary objective of this course is to provide students with a comprehensive understanding of the theory of functions of complex variables, their properties, and applications in various fields such as physics and engineering.

**Prerequisites**

To successfully engage with this course, students are required to have completed "Real Analysis - I" and "Multivariable Calculus." These foundational courses ensure that students possess the necessary mathematical maturity and background to delve into the intricacies of complex analysis. While no other pre-requisites are specified at this time, it is advisable for interested students to consult with academic advisors or course coordinators for further guidance.

**Learning Outcomes**

By the end of this course, students will be able to:

1. **CO1:** Demonstrate a thorough understanding of the geometry of complex numbers and their properties.
2. **CO2:** Analyze functions of a complex variable, compute their derivatives, integrals, and explore concepts like singularities and zeros.
3. **CO3:** Evaluate complicated real integrals using residue calculus techniques.
4. **CO4:** Apply the theory of complex analysis to solve problems in physics and engineering.
5. **CO5:** Understand and utilize conformal mapping for various applications.

**Weekly Syllabus and Topics**

The course is structured into 13 weeks, each focusing on specific topics with corresponding homework assignments:

- **Weeks 1 & 2:** Review of complex numbers and analytic functions; introduction to derivatives in the complex plane.
- **Weeks 3 & 4:** Exploration of hyperbolic functions and contour integrals.
- **Weeks 5 & 6:** Study of branch cuts, antiderivatives, Cauchy-Goursat theorem, and Cauchy Integral formula.
- **Weeks 7 & 8:** Examination of power series expansions, convergence, and integration/differentiation techniques.
- **Weeks 9 & 10:** Discussion on singularities, residues, and applications of the residue theorem.
- **Weeks 11 & 12:** Advanced topics including Jordan’s Lemma, Argument principle, Rouché’s Theorem, and conformal mapping.
- **Week 13:** Comprehensive review for end-of-term examination.

**Assignments**

Each week includes homework assignments designed to reinforce the concepts covered in lectures. These assignments are labeled as "HW X," where X corresponds to the week number.

**Assessment Strategy**

The course assessment is structured as follows:

- **Homework (30%):** Regular assignments contribute significantly to the final grade, emphasizing consistent practice and understanding.
- **Mid-Semester Examination (20%):** A comprehensive evaluation of foundational concepts covered in the first half of the course.
- **Tutorials/Viva/Presentation (10%):** Assessment through interactive sessions, supplementary explanations, or presentations.
- **End-Semester Examination (40%):** A cumulative assessment covering the entire syllabus, reflecting overall understanding and application skills.

Additional evaluations may be included at the discretion of the course instructor to cover any unaccounted aspects of student performance.
