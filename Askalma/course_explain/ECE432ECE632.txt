### Course Overview  
This course, **ECE432 / ECE632: Radar Systems**, is designed for senior undergraduate and graduate students in electrical and computer engineering. It provides a comprehensive introduction to the principles of radar systems, focusing on their design, operation, and applications. The course combines theoretical concepts with practical applications, emphasizing key topics such as radar cross-section, clutter, and signal processing techniques.

---

### Prerequisites  
To successfully engage with this course, students are expected to have completed **ECE 201: Signals and Systems**. Familiarity with **MATLAB** is highly desirable, as it will be used for assignments and simulations throughout the course.

---

### Course Outcomes (COs)  
By the end of this course, students will be able to:  
- **CO1**: Derive radar systems parameters such as bandwidth, carrier frequency, waveform, and power to achieve specific radar characteristics.  
- **CO2**: Process radar signals to estimate range, velocity, and angular positions of targets.  
- **CO3**: Explain the functionalities of common radar applications, including military, remote sensing, and automotive uses.

### Assessment Plan  

The course assessment is designed to evaluate both theoretical understanding and practical application of radar systems. The breakdown is as follows:  
- **Homework (15%)**: Regular assignments to reinforce concepts covered in lectures.  
- **Quiz (10%)**: Short assessments to test understanding of key topics.  
- **Mid-Semester Examination (25%)**: Comprehensive evaluation of material covered in the first half of the course.  
- **Project (20%)**: An individual project involving radar system design and implementation, with a final presentation.  
- **End-Semester Examination (30%)**: A cumulative assessment covering the entire course content.

The end-semester examination carries a significant weightage due to its importance in evaluating overall understanding of radar systems and their applications.

### Resource Materials  

#### Textbooks:  
1. **"High Resolution Radar"** by David Werner  
   This textbook provides a detailed overview of modern radar systems, focusing on high-resolution techniques and their applications.  

#### References:  
1. **"Introduction to Radar Systems, 3rd edition"** by M. Skolnik  
   A comprehensive reference covering the fundamentals of radar systems, including signal processing and system design.  
2. **"Radar Cross Section, SciTech Publishing"** by E. F. Knott, J. F. Shaeffer, and M. T. Tuley  
   This book delves into the principles of radar cross-section analysis, a critical aspect of radar system design.
