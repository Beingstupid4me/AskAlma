### Course Overview  
The course titled **Integrated Circuit Fabrication (ECE 522)** is a fundamental course for both undergraduate and postgraduate students pursuing degrees in electrical engineering or related fields. This course provides a comprehensive understanding of the physical and chemical processes involved in the fabrication of silicon-based integrated circuits. It covers topics ranging from crystal growth to advanced fabrication techniques such as lithography, etching, diffusion, ion implantation, and packaging. The course is designed for students who have a quantitative background in device physics, particularly those familiar with diodes, bipolar junction transistors (BJTs), and field-effect transistors (FETs).  

### Prerequisites  
The prerequisite for this course includes a solid understanding of **Solid State Devices & Semiconductor Physics**. Students are expected to have prior knowledge of the basic principles governing semiconductor devices, including their structure, operation, and material properties. Desirable pre-requisites may include exposure to advanced topics in device physics or integrated circuit design.

### Learning Outcomes (COs)  
By the end of this course, students will be able to:  

- **CO1:** Identify the IC production cycle and describe semiconductor materials and their defects.  
- **CO2:** Determine and develop process parameters for thermal oxidation, metallization, photolithography, etching, diffusion, and ion implantation processes.  
- **CO3:** Design the ion implantation process, identify implant damage, and implement techniques to remove such damage.  
- **CO4:** Generate the flow of IC fabrication processes and design specifications for fabricated resistors, capacitors, and inductors.  
- **CO5:** Plan processing steps for BJT, MOSFET, BiCMOS, BEOL, SOI, FinFET, and MESFET fabrication and choose appropriate packaging techniques.
