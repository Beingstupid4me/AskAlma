### Course Overview  
This course CSE529, titled "Approximation Algorithm" is an advanced exploration into the design and analysis of approximation algorithms, focusing on NP-hard problems commonly encountered in fields such as communication, transportation, and resource allocation. It is designed for both undergraduate and postgraduate students, providing them with a robust foundation in algorithmic techniques and their applications.

---

### Prerequisites  
Students enrolling in this course are expected to have completed **CSE222 ADA** (Data Structures and Algorithms) and **Grad Algorithms** (Advanced Algorithms). While **CSE222 ADA** provides essential background in algorithm design, **Grad Algorithms** is particularly crucial as it introduces students to advanced algorithmic concepts that will be built upon in this course. ### Learning Outcomes  
By the end of this course, students will be able to:  
- **CO1:** Design and analyze approximation algorithms for NP-hard problems.  
- **CO2:** Apply mathematical tools to evaluate the worst-case performance of these algorithms.  
- **CO3:** Analyze and prove the limitations of approximability for various optimization problems.  
- **CO4:** Understand advanced techniques such as primal-dual methods, iterative rounding, and semidefinite programming in the context of approximation algorithms.

### Assessment Strategy  
The course is assessed through a combination of evaluations designed to measure both theoretical understanding and practical application. The breakdown is as follows:  
- **Mid-Semester Examination (30%)**: Covers foundational concepts and algorithms introduced in the first half of the course.  
- **Assignments (10%)**: Includes problem sets that reinforce lecture topics and encourage independent exploration of approximation techniques.  
- **Quizzes (20%)**: Regular assessments to evaluate understanding of key concepts and recent material.  
- **End-Semester Examination (40%)**: Comprehensive evaluation of all course content, emphasizing the ability to apply learned algorithms to complex problems.

Additional evaluations may be included at the discretion of the instructor if necessary.
