### Course Overview  
The course SSH214 titled "Introduction To The Study Of Literature" is a 4-credit course offered by the Department of English. It is designed for undergraduate students (UG) to provide them with an in-depth understanding of literature as a window into society. This course aims to expose students to various literary genres, including poetry, drama, novels, and short stories, while also encouraging critical thinking about common social issues explored by writers from diverse backgrounds and cultures.

### Prerequisites  
The course does not specify any prerequisites beyond the general eligibility for UG studies. Students are expected to have a basic understanding of reading and writing and an interest in exploring different forms of literature.

### Learning Outcomes (COs)  
By the end of this course, students will be able to:  
- **CO1:** Understand how literature reflects and shapes societal values and issues.  
- **CO2:** Analyze literary texts using critical methods across various genres such as prose, poetry, and drama.  
- **CO3:** Identify and appreciate themes in literature that address common social issues examined by writers from diverse backgrounds.  
- **CO4:** Engage in creative writing activities and participate in group discussions to explore the intersection of literature with cultural and societal contexts.

### Assessment Strategy  
The course employs a comprehensive assessment strategy to evaluate students' understanding and application of literary concepts:  

- **Assignments:** 20% Contribution  
  Includes weekly assignments, participation in creative writing activities, and group projects.

- **Midterm Exam:** 20% Contribution  
  A written exam assessing students' grasp of literary analysis techniques covered in the first half of the course.

- **Surprise Tests:** 10% Contribution  
  Periodic surprise quizzes to evaluate students' understanding of weekly topics.

- **End Semester Exam:** 40% Contribution  
  A comprehensive written exam covering all topics discussed during the semester, emphasizing critical analysis and application of literary concepts.

- **Participation:** 10% Contribution  
  Active participation in class discussions, group activities, and creative writing sessions is essential for earning full marks.
