### Course Overview

This course DES506, titled **Advanced Topics in Human Centered Computing (ATHC)**, is designed for both undergraduate and postgraduate students. It carries 4 credits and provides an advanced understanding of current-day Human Centered Computing (HCC) frameworks and research methodologies. The primary objective of this course is to equip students with the ability to conduct research in the area of HCC.

The course description highlights that it will cover existing frameworks, research methodologies, and their practical applications. Students will learn how to apply these frameworks and methodologies to real-world problems. The course also emphasizes the importance of understanding the critical flaws in computing and design solutions and proposing alternative approaches to address these issues.

---

### Prerequisites

While there are no mandatory prerequisites explicitly listed for this course, it is recommended that students have a foundational knowledge of Human Centered Computing (HCC). Specifically, **HCD 204** (Design of Interactive Systems) is strongly encouraged as a prerequisite. Students who lack this background may find the course challenging and are advised to consult with the instructor if they have any doubts.

---

### Learning Outcomes

By the end of this course, students will be able to:

1. **CO1**: Demonstrate a deep understanding of current-day HCC frameworks and research methodologies.
2. **CO2**: Identify and apply suitable research frameworks for given problems while critiquing and justifying their use.
3. **CO3**: Develop research hypotheses and generate innovative ideas related to research problems in HCC.
4. **CO4**: Evaluate existing computing and design solutions, critically analyzing their methodology, experimentation, evaluation, and propose alternative approaches to address any critical flaws.

---

### Weekly Lecture Plan

The course is divided into 12 weeks, each focusing on specific topics that build upon the learning outcomes:

- **Week 1**: Introduction to HCC frameworks and research methodologies. This foundational week sets the stage for understanding how these frameworks are applied in real-world scenarios.
- **Week 2**: Feminist HCI Lens Methodology. Students will learn about the application of feminist principles in Human Centered Computing.
- **Week 3**: Feminist HCI Applications. Building on the previous week, students will explore practical applications of feminist principles in HCC.
- **Weeks 4–5**: DBIR Methodology and Applications. These weeks focus on Design-Based Iterative Research (DBIR) methodologies and their practical implementations.
- **Weeks 6–7**: Aspiration-based design Methodology and Applications. Students will learn about aspiration-based approaches and how they are applied in HCC.
- **Weeks 8–9**: Asset-based design Methodology and Applications. This section covers asset-based approaches and their real-world applications.
- **Weeks 10–11**: HCC for Development. These weeks focus on the application of HCC principles in development contexts, with both general topics and specific case studies.
- **Weeks 12–13**: Emerging Themes in HCC. This section explores cutting-edge topics and trends in HCC, preparing students for advanced research.
- **Week 14**: Emerging Themes in HCC (continued). The final week allows for deeper exploration of emerging themes and their implications.

Each week includes assignments that reinforce the concepts covered in lectures, ensuring a hands-on approach to learning.

---

### Assessment Plan

The course assessment is designed to evaluate both theoretical knowledge and practical application:

- **Assignments**: 60% of the grade. These include critical reviews of research papers, which account for 15 out of 20 submissions contributing to 60 marks.
- **Class Participation**: 0% of the grade. While attendance is mandatory, participation does not contribute to the final grade but is encouraged for a better understanding of the material.
- **Project**: 30% of the grade. Students will work on an in-depth project involving reading and analyzing research papers or exploring new research areas.

---

### Resource Material

The course provides comprehensive resource materials to support learning:

- **Textbook**: *Research Methods in Human Centered Computing* by Dr. Anurag Desai.
- **Reference Materials**: Research papers from journals such as CHI, CSCW, ToCHI, UIST, and UbiComp.

These resources are essential for understanding the theoretical foundations and practical applications of HCC.

---

### Anti-Plagiarism Guidelines

Plagiarism is strictly prohibited. Students are encouraged to refer to external resources or discuss ideas with peers but must ensure all submitted work is 100% original. Proper documentation and citations are required, and any form of academic dishonesty will be dealt with according to the Institute's guidelines.

---

### Class Attendance Policy

Regular attendance is mandatory, with a penalty system in place for absences. Students who are late by more than 5 minutes or absent without prior notice (accompanied by Institute-validated proof) will receive an absence mark. Missing classes results in a penalty of -1 per absence, with the maximum penalty capped at -20.

---

This structured approach ensures that students gain a comprehensive understanding of Human Centered Computing frameworks and methodologies while preparing them for advanced research and practical applications in the field.
