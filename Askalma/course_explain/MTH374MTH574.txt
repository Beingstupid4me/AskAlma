### Course Overview  
This course, **MTH374/MTH574: Linear Optimisation**, is designed to introduce students to the application of optimization techniques in various domains within Computer Science and Engineering (CSE) and Electronics and Communication Engineering (ECE). The primary focus will be on linear optimization, also known as linear programming, where students will learn about the structural and algorithmic aspects of solving optimization problems. The course is divided into weekly lecture plans that cover theoretical concepts, computational methods, and practical applications. Additionally, the course includes assignments and assessments to reinforce learning outcomes.

---

### Prerequisites  
The prerequisite for this course is **Linear Algebra**, which provides a foundational understanding of vectors, matrices, and systems of linear equations. Desirable prerequisites include an introduction to programming, as students will be expected to implement algorithms and solve real-world problems using computational tools.

---

### Learning Outcomes (COs)  
By the end of this course, students will be able to:  
- **CO1**: Formulate Linear/Non-Linear optimization problems in their standard mathematical form.  
- **CO2**: Apply algorithms such as the simplex and interior point methods to solve optimization problems efficiently.  
- **CO3**: Use solvers to obtain solutions for moderately sized problems, including optimal solutions or lower bounds.  
- **CO4**: Understand and apply concepts related to convex programs and semi-definite programs (SDPs) with real-world applications.

### Assessment Strategy  
The course assessment is designed to evaluate both theoretical understanding and practical application of optimization techniques. The breakdown is as follows:  

- **Assignments**: 40% (Includes a mix of theory-based and coding-based exercises)  
- **Quiz**: 25% (In-class quizzes to assess understanding of key concepts)  
- **Mid-Semester Examination**: 25% (Comprehensive evaluation of the first half of the course)  
- **End-Semester Examination**: 35% (Final assessment covering all topics discussed during the semester)

The weightage of each component is carefully considered to ensure a balanced evaluation of student performance.
