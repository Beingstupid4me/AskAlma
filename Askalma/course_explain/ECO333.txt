Course Overview  
This course ECO333, named "Derivatives and Risk Management" provides an in-depth exploration of financial risk management with a strong focus on derivatives. Designed for undergraduate students, it combines theoretical knowledge with practical applications to ensure comprehensive understanding.

Prerequisites  
Students must have completed either ECO331 (Foundations of Finance) or ECO332 (Valuation and Portfolio Management) to successfully engage with this course.

Learning Outcomes  
By the end of this course, students will be able to:  
- Understand the concept of risk in finance.  
- Analyze and apply principles of option pricing and strategies in managing financial risks.  
- Evaluate and implement hedging strategies using derivative instruments to mitigate financial risks.  
- Understand the structure and applications of forwards, futures, swaps, and other derivatives.  

Weekly Syllabus and Topics  
The course is divided into 13 weeks:  
- Weeks 1-2: Introduction to risk concepts and derivative instruments.  
- Weeks 3-6: Options market and strategies.  
- Weeks 7-9: Principles of option pricing using Black-Scholes model.  
- Weeks 10-11: Forwards, futures, swaps, and hedging strategies.  
- Week 12: Weather, energy, and commodity derivatives.  

Assessment Strategy  
The course employs a comprehensive assessment strategy with quizzes (4 at 10% each), projects (20%), and an end-term examination (40%). This structure ensures both continuous evaluation and comprehensive understanding are emphasized.

Learning Resources  
Students have access to textbooks such as "Options, Futures, and Other Derivatives" by John C. Hull and online resources like www.cbotc.com for supplementary learning.

Conclusion  
This course equips students with the theoretical knowledge and practical skills necessary for addressing challenges in finance. Emphasizing real-world applications ensures students are well-prepared for their future careers.
