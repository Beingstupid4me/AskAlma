**Course Overview**

The course, coded SSH216 and titled "Introduction to Indian Mythology (IIM)," is a 2-credit course offered to undergraduate students. Its primary objective is to introduce students to the vast and rich world of Indian mythology, with a focus on understanding stories as tools for exploring truth and developing perspective.

**Prerequisites**

While there are no mandatory prerequisites listed, students are encouraged to have a basic understanding of historical contexts and cultural narratives. Additionally, "None specified" for other prerequisites means that no further requirements are needed beyond the course's general expectations.

**Learning Outcomes (Course Outcomes)**

By the end of this course, students will be able to:

- **CO1:** Discuss and critique fundamental concepts related to myth, mythology, history, and their interrelations.
- **CO2:** Analyze specific characters and events from Mahabharat and Ramayana with depth and critical thinking.
- **CO3:** Correlate and distinguish between the two major epics of Indian mythology: Mahabharata and Ramayana.

**Weekly Lecture Plan**

The course is structured into 12 weekly lectures, each focusing on specific topics that align with the learning outcomes. Below is a detailed breakdown:

1. **Week 1:** Introduction to the course.
   - Focuses on familiarizing students with the course structure, objectives, and evaluation criteria.

2. **Week 2:** Myth, Mythology, History – Understanding terminologies.
   - Explores foundational concepts necessary for understanding Indian mythology.

3. **Week 3:** Origin Myths in Hindu Mythology (CO1 completed).
   - Discusses key origin myths and their significance.

4. **Week 4:** Overview of the Ramayana.
   - Provides an introduction to one of the most significant epics, focusing on its structure and themes.

5. **Week 5:** The story of Valmiki (CO2 - half done).
   - Delves into the life and contributions of Valmiki.

6. **Week 6:** Birth of Ram and Sita – The Lore and Symbolism (CO1 completed).
   - Examines the creation myth of Ram and Sita, highlighting their significance.

7. **Week 7:** Ravana – Character Analysis.
   - Analyzes the character of Ravana and his role in the epic.

8. **Week 8:** Mahabharata – Overview (CO2 completed).
   - Offers an introduction to the structure and themes of Mahabharata.

9. **Week 9:** Shakuntala and Dushyanta.
   - Focuses on key characters from Mahabharata, exploring their roles and significance.

10. **Week 10:** Dyut Sabha and Disrobing of Draupadi (CO3 completed).
    - Examines political themes and character dynamics in this segment.

11. **Week 11:** Analysis of the War.
    - Explores significant events, such as the Chakravyuha war, and their implications.

12. **Week 12:** Comparison of the two epics (continued into lecture 13).
    - Concludes with a comparative analysis of Mahabharata and Ramayana to reinforce learning outcomes.

**Assessment Plan**

The course employs a comprehensive assessment strategy to evaluate student understanding and progress:

- **Assignment:** 20% contribution, designed to encourage critical thinking and application of concepts.
- **Mid-sem:** 25% weightage, assessing students' grasp of the first half of the course material.
- **Class Participation:** 5% weightage, recognizing active engagement in discussions and activities.
- **End-sem:** 50% contribution, a comprehensive examination evaluating overall understanding.

**Learning Resources**

Students are encouraged to utilize the following resources for their studies:

- **Textbook:** Comprehensive reading materials that cover all aspects of Indian mythology.
- **Reference Book:** Additional readings recommended by the instructor for deeper insights into specific topics.

A detailed reading list will be provided at the beginning of the course, ensuring students have access to all necessary materials.
