### Course Overview  
This course is an introduction to contemporary social and political philosophy with a focus on Western Political Theory and Indian political theoretical traditions. It covers fundamental debates in areas such as Utilitarianism, Liberal Equality, Libertarianism, Marxism, Communitarianism, Multiculturism, Feminism, and their relevance within the Indian context. The course is designed for undergraduate students (UG) and carries 4 academic credits.  

### Prerequisites  
- **Mandatory Prerequisites**: None specified.  
- **Desirable Prerequisites**: None specified.  
- **Other Prerequisites**: None specified.  

### Learning Outcomes (COs)  
By the end of this course, students will be able to:  
1. **CO1**: Understand and critically engage with key historical and contemporary debates in Western social and political philosophy.  
2. **CO2**: Understand and critically engage with key debates in Indian political theory.  
3. **CO3**: Recognize and evaluate similar theories while analyzing relevant political arguments.  
4. **CO4**: Make normative arguments, develop personal views, and apply them to social and political contexts.  
5. **CO5**: Critically evaluate arguments through participation in group discussions and debates.  

### Weekly Syllabus and Topics  
The course is divided into 13 weeks, each focusing on specific topics:  

- **Week 1**: Introduction to Social and Political Philosophy - Key Questions (CO-1).  
- **Week 2**: Utilitarianism (CO-1, CO-3, CO-4).  
- **Week 3**: Liberal Equality (CO-1, CO-3, CO-4).  
- **Week 4**: Libertarianism (CO-1, CO-3, CO-4, CO-5) with a Response paper.  
- **Week 5**: Marxism (CO-1, CO-3, CO-4, CO-5) with a Response paper.  
- **Week 6**: Communitarianism (CO-1, CO-3, CO-4).  
- **Week 7**: Multiculturalism (CO-1, CO-3, CO-4) with Assignments.  
- **Week 8**: Feminism (CO-1, CO-3, CO-4) with Assignments.  
- **Week 9**: What is Indian Political Theory (IPT)? (CO-2).  
- **Week 10**: Debates on Equality in IPT (CO-2, CO-3, CO-4).  
- **Week 11**: Debates on Freedom in IPT (CO-2, CO-3, CO-4).  
- **Week 12**: Debates on Religion in IPT (CO-2, CO-3, CO-4, CO-5) with Presentations.  
- **Week 13**: Revisions and Presentations (CO-4, CO-5) with Presentations.  

### Assessment Strategy  
The course assessment is divided as follows:  
- **Assignments**: 30% of the grade.  
- **Response papers**: 20% of the grade.  
- **Presentations**: 25% of the grade.  
- **Online participation**: 5% of the grade.  
- **End semester examination**: 20% of the grade.  

### Learning Resources  
The course provides comprehensive learning resources, including:  
1. **Textbook**: Kymlicka, W. (2002). *Contemporary Political Philosophy: An Introduction*. New York: OUP.  
2. **Reference Book 1**: Mahajan, G.(2013). *India: Political Ideas and the making of the democratic discourse*. New York: Zed Books.  
3. **Reference Book 2**: Singh, A. & Mohapatra, S. (2010). *Indian Political Thought: A Reader*. Routledge.

- Assignments for Week 3 to Week 8 are detailed in the weekly lecture plan.
