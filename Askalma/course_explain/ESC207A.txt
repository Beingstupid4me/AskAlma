**Course Overview**

This course ESC207A, titled "Ecology, Evolution, and Environment" provides an in-depth exploration of ecology, evolution, and environmental science. Designed for undergraduate students, it combines foundational concepts with practical applications to foster a holistic understanding of environmental issues and biodiversity conservation.

**Prerequisites**

Students should have completed high school biology as the prerequisite for this course.

**Learning Outcomes (COs)**

By the end of this course, students will be able to:

- **CO1:** Analyze human-induced factors contributing to environmental degradation.
- **CO2:** Evaluate the importance and necessity of biodiversity conservation.
- **CO3:** Generate hypotheses on how ecosystem services might be affected by climate change.
- **CO4:** Understand and apply principles of evolution in ecological contexts.
- **CO5:** Develop strategies for sustainable practices based on ecological knowledge.

**Weekly Lecture Plan**

- **Week 1:** Introduction to ecology, evolution, and environmental science. Overview of course objectives and significance of biodiversity conservation.
- **Weeks 2-5:** Foundational concepts in evolution, including natural selection and genetic variation.
- **Weeks 6-9:** Fundamentals of ecology, focusing on populations, communities, and ecosystems.
- **Week 10:** Ecosystem dynamics, including energy flow and nutrient cycling.
- **Week 11:** Fieldwork introduction and practical applications of ecological concepts.
- **Week 12:** Biodiversity conservation strategies and practices.
- **Weeks 13:** Current environmental challenges and their implications for conservation efforts.

**Assignments**

Regular assignments are integrated into the weekly lectures to reinforce learning. These include group projects, laboratory exercises, and individual reflections on course topics.

**Assessment Plan**

The course assessment is structured as follows:

- **Mid-Semester Exam (30%):** Covers foundational concepts in evolution and ecology.
- **End-Semester Exam (30%):** Assesses comprehensive understanding of biodiversity conservation and environmental challenges.
- **Course Project (20%):** A group project addressing a specific environmental issue, requiring research, analysis, and presentation.
- **Assignments (10%):** Weekly assignments designed to enhance practical skills and application of course concepts.
- **Quiz (10%):** Short quizzes to assess understanding of key topics covered in lectures.

**Resource Materials**

The primary textbooks for this course are:

1. *From Individuals to Ecosystems* by Michael Begon, Colin R. Townsend, and John L. Harper.
2. *The Economy of Nature* by R.E. Ricklefs.
3. *Evolution* by D.J. Futuyama.
4. *The Ecology of the Changing Planet* by Mark B. Bush.

These texts provide a comprehensive foundation for understanding ecological principles and their applications.

**Learning Resources**

Students are encouraged to utilize library resources, online databases, and academic journals to supplement their learning. Additional materials will be provided as needed.

This course is designed to equip students with the knowledge and skills necessary to address complex environmental challenges effectively. By integrating theoretical concepts with practical applications, students will gain a well-rounded understanding of ecology and its relevance to global sustainability efforts.
