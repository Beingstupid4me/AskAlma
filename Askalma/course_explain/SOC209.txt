**Course Overview: SOC209 - Consumer Culture and Society**

This course, SOC209 - Consumer Culture and Society, is designed to explore the profound impact of consumerism on contemporary societies. The course delves into how consumption shapes individuals' identities, social structures, and cultural practices. Students will examine key concepts such as modernity, identity, inequality, lifestyle, freedom, power, and signification within various consumption domains, including food, fashion, media, and intimate relations. Additionally, the course critically evaluates consumerism's role in addressing contemporary issues like social, political, and ecological challenges.

**Prerequisites:**
To enroll in SOC209, students must have successfully completed SOC201. This prerequisite ensures that participants have a foundational understanding of societal concepts relevant to consumer culture.

**Course Outcomes (CO):**
By the end of this course, students will be able to:
- **CO1:** Identify key concepts of consumerism and consumption and their relevance in modern society.
- **CO2:** Discuss consumption practices through an intersectional analysis lens.
- **CO3:** Analyze specific consumption practices using self-reflexive exercises.
- **CO4:** Develop the skill to examine contemporary social, political, and ecological debates about commodification and consumerism.

**Weekly Lecture Plan:**
The course is structured over 13 weeks, with each week focusing on a specific topic that builds upon the previous one. Some weeks include assignments or assessments, while others are reserved for student presentations based on individual research projects.

- **Week 1:** Introduction to Consumer Culture and Global Forces
- **Week 2:** Consumerism and Identity
- **Week 3:** Social Stratification and Consumerism
- **Week 4:** Conspicuous Consumption in Everyday Life
- **Week 5:** Lifestyle Practices and Society
- **Week 6:** Subjectivity and Reflexivity: Race, Gender, Class Dimensions in Consumption
- **Week 7:** Commodification and Material Culture
- **Week 8:** Image Economy: Fashion, Cosmetics, and Body
- **Week 9:** Media and Its Effect on Consumption Practices
- **Week 10:** Food Practices and Ecological Concerns
- **Week 11:** Responsible Consumption and Waste Management
- **Week 12:** Anti-Consumption Strategies: Wave of Minimalistic Lifestyle
- **Week 13:** Student Presentations

**Assessment Plan:**
The course employs a multifaceted assessment strategy to evaluate student learning effectively:
- **In-class Assessment (ICA):** Contributes 10% to the final grade.
- **Field Visit and Presentation:** Worth 30%, this component encourages students to apply theoretical knowledge in real-world contexts.
- **Take-Home Assignments (THA):** Comprises 20% of the final grade, allowing students to reflect on course concepts independently.
- **Mid-Semester Exam:** Holds a weightage of 20%, assessing students' understanding halfway through the course.
- **End-Semester Exam:** Contributes the remaining 20%, providing a comprehensive evaluation of the entire syllabus.

**Resource Materials:**
The primary textbooks for this course are:
1. *Consumer Culture (2nd edition)* by Celia Lury, published by Rutgers University Press.
2. *The Consumer Society Reader* by M.J. Lee, published by Blackwell.

Additional resources will be assigned by the instructor to supplement these texts and provide students with a well-rounded understanding of consumer culture.

---

This structured approach ensures that students gain a comprehensive understanding of consumer culture and its societal implications, while also developing critical skills in analysis, reflection, and application through various assessments.
