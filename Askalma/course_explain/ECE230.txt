### Course Overview  
The course ECE230, titled "Fields and Waves" is a core component of the undergraduate curriculum for second-year engineering students. It carries 4 academic credits and is offered during the second year, spanning both semesters. The course provides an in-depth introduction to electrodynamics, including electrostatics, magnetostatics, electrodynamics, and electromagnetic wave propagation. These fundamental concepts are essential for students interested in fields such as radio frequency (RF), microwave, millimeter-wave, and optical systems.

#### Prerequisites  
Students enrolling in this course must have successfully completed the prerequisite course MTH203. No additional prerequisites or recommended courses are specified beyond this requirement.

### Learning Outcomes  
By the end of this course, students will be able to:  

1. **CO1**: Execute vector calculus operations (gradient, divergence, curl, line, surface, and volume integrals) in Cartesian and curvilinear coordinates.  
2. **CO2**: Apply Coulomb's law, Gauss's law, and Poisson's equation to solve for electric field, flux density, and potentials for charge distributions in various configurations.  
3. **CO3**: Utilize Biot-Savart's law and Ampere's law to determine magnetic fields and flux densities in different scenarios.  
4. **CO4**: Analyze Maxwell's equations of time-harmonic fields to describe electromagnetic wave propagation in diverse media and boundary conditions.  
5. **CO5**: Evaluate electromagnetic wave propagation through simple transmission lines.

### Weekly Lecture Plan  
The course is divided into 21 weeks, with each week focusing on specific topics that align with the learning outcomes:  

- **Week 1**: Vector Analysis (Vector algebra, differential calculus, integral calculus, curvilinear coordinates) - CO1.  
- **Week 2**: Electrostatics (Coulomb's law, electric field, Gauss's law, electric potential, energy and potential, electric fields in matter) - CO2.  
- **Week 3**: Magnetostatics (Biot-Savart's law, magnetic field, Ampere's law, magnetic fields in matter) - CO3.  
- **Week 4**: Electrodynamics (Electromotive force, electromagnetic induction, Maxwell's equations) - CO4.  
- **Week 5**: Electromagnetic Waves Propagation (Helmholtz wave equation, electromagnetic waves in vacuum and matter, Poynting vector, reflection/transmission at normal incidence) - CO4.  
- **Week 6**: Transmission Lines Equations and Parameters (Time domain and frequency domain analysis, standing wave ratio) - CO5.  

No specific topics or assignments are mentioned for Weeks 7 through 21.

### Assessment Strategy  
The course assessment is designed to evaluate both theoretical understanding and practical application:  

- **Midsem Exam**: 30% contribution, focusing on foundational concepts covered in the first half of the semester.  
- **Endsem Exam**: 35% contribution, emphasizing comprehensive understanding of the entire syllabus.  
- **Quiz**: 15% contribution, conducted periodically to assess regular progress and grasp of key topics.  
- **Homework Assignments**: 20% contribution, reinforcing concepts through problem-solving exercises assigned weekly.

Additional assessment methods may be included if specified by the instructor.
