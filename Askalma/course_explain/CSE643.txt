### Course Overview  
The course titled **Artificial Intelligence (CSE643)** is a 4-credit course offered to both undergraduate (UG) and postgraduate (PG) students. This course provides an in-depth introduction to Artificial Intelligence, covering fundamental concepts such as search techniques, knowledge representation, reasoning, and learning. The primary objective of this course is to equip students with the ability to design and implement intelligent systems that can solve complex problems.

---

### Prerequisites  
The prerequisite for this course includes a basic understanding of **CSE102 Data Structures and Algorithms** and **CSE121 Discrete Mathematics**. These foundational courses are required as they provide the necessary background in problem-solving, algorithmic thinking, and mathematical reasoning, which are essential for success in this course.

---

### Learning Outcomes (COs)  
By the end of this course, students will be able to:  
- **CO1**: Apply basic search techniques for problem-solving.  
- **CO2**: Explain how knowledge representation is used for problem-solving.  
- **CO3**: Apply reasoning methods to analyze and sift through data.  
- **CO4**: Utilize AI technologies in real-world applications.

### Assessment Strategy  
The course assessment is designed to evaluate both theoretical understanding and practical application of the concepts covered. The evaluation components and their weightages are as follows:  

| Type of Evaluation         | % Contribution in Grade |
|----------------------------|------------------------|
| Quiz                       | 15                     |
| Assignment                  | 35                     |
| Mid-sem Examination        | 25                     |
| End-sem Examination       | 25                     |

The end-semester examination carries a significant weightage of **50%**, reflecting its importance in evaluating overall understanding.
