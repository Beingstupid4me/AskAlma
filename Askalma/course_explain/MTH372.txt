### Course Overview  
The course titled **Statistical Inference (MTH372)** is a 4-credit undergraduate/graduate program designed to provide students with a comprehensive understanding of parametric and non-parametric statistical inference. The course also introduces foundational concepts in building statistical linear models, focusing on estimation, hypothesis testing, confidence intervals, and the application of linear regression and ANOVA techniques. Students will gain hands-on experience through simulations, real data analysis, and practical assignments using tools like MATLAB.

---

### Prerequisites  
Students enrolling in this course are expected to have completed **Probability and Statistics** (MTH201) or an equivalent foundational course in probability theory. Additionally, a basic understanding of statistical signal processing or econometrics is recommended. Students who have completed **Statistical Signal Processing/Econometrics I** (MTH356) will find this course particularly beneficial.

---

### Learning Outcomes  
By the end of this course, students will be able to:  
1. **CO1**: Understand and apply principles of data reduction, including sufficient, minimal, and complete statistics, as well as likelihood and invariance principles.  
2. **CO2**: Master methods of point estimation, such as moments, maximum likelihood, and expectation minimization, while evaluating estimators based on criteria like mean squared error, unbiasedness, sufficiency, and consistency.  
3. **CO3**: Conduct hypothesis testing using techniques like the likelihood ratio test, invariant tests, power functions, and most powerful tests.  
4. **CO4**: Construct confidence intervals for known probability distributions and apply these methods to real-world data analysis.  
5. **CO5**: Build and analyze simple statistical models, including linear regression and ANOVA, using appropriate software tools.

### Assessment Strategy  
The course assessment is designed to evaluate both theoretical understanding and practical application. The breakdown is as follows:  
- **Assignments**: 20% (Includes problem-solving tasks and real-world data analysis)  
- **Quiz**: 20% (Evaluates understanding of key concepts covered in lectures)  
- **Mid-Semester Examination**: 25% (Assesses comprehensive grasp of course material up to the midpoint)  
- **End-Semester Examination**: 35% (Comprehensive evaluation of all topics covered during the semester)

Additional assessment types, such as lab reports or group projects, may be included at the discretion of the instructor.
