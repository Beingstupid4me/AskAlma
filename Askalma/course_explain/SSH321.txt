**Course Overview**

This course is an introduction to Applied Ethics (SSH321), designed for undergraduate students. It provides a comprehensive understanding of fundamental ethical theories, moral dilemmas, and their applications in contemporary society. The course emphasizes critical thinking, analysis, and argumentation to justify ethical claims.

---

**Prerequisites**

Students enrolling in this course must have completed the following prerequisites:
- **Pre-requisites (Mandatory):** None specified.
- **Pre-requisites (Desired):** Basic understanding of philosophy and logical reasoning.
- **Pre-requisites (Other):** None specified.

---

**Learning Outcomes**

By the end of this course, students will be able to:
1. **CO1:** Understand and recognize key theories and issues in applied ethics.
2. **CO2:** Apply ethical principles and precepts to contemporary ethical concerns.
3. **CO3:** Demonstrate clarity, precision, and accuracy in reasoning, understand ethical dilemmas, and avoid fallacies in reasoning.
4. **CO4:** Explain, evaluate, and defend philosophical positions.

**Assessment Strategy**

The course employs a comprehensive assessment strategy to evaluate student learning:

- **Mid-Term Examination:** 15% of the final grade.
- **Response papers (Weekly):** 20% of the final grade. Students are required to submit one response paper per week, except for Week 3 and Week 6, where no specific assignments were detailed in the provided data.
- **Project:** 35% of the final grade. The project is a significant component of the assessment, allowing students to apply their knowledge to real-world scenarios.
- **Class Participation:** 10% of the final grade. Active participation in discussions and activities is encouraged.
- **End-of-Semester Examination:** 20% of the final grade.

**Conclusion**

This course offers a comprehensive exploration of applied ethics, equipping students with the theoretical knowledge and practical skills to address complex ethical dilemmas. Through a combination of lectures, readings, assignments, and assessments, students will gain a deep understanding of the subject matter and its relevance to contemporary issues.
