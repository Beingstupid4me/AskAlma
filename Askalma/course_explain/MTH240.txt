Course Overview  
This course MTH240, named "Real Analysis I" provides an in-depth exploration of real analysis, single-variable calculus, and multivariable calculus with vector calculus concepts. It begins with the real number system, sequences, series, differentiation, integration, and extends to functions of several variables, including multiple integrals.

**Course Outcomes (COs):**

1. **CO1:** Students will understand the properties of the real number system, including supremum, infimum, completeness axiom, Archimedean property, and density.
2. **CO2:** Students will apply differentiation and integration techniques to solve problems involving sequences and series.
3. **CO3:** Students will analyze functions of several variables, evaluate integrals in multiple dimensions, and apply calculus principles to geometric problems.

**Weekly Lecture Plan:**

- **Week 1:** Real Number System (Lectures: 3)  
  Topics include countable sets, supremum, infimum, completeness axiom, Archimedean property.
  
- **Weeks 2–4:** Sequences and Series of Real Numbers (Lectures: 9)  
  Covers convergence/divergence, bounded sequences, Cauchy sequences, Bolzano-Weierstrass theorem, limit superior/inferior, alternating series.

- **Weeks 5–9:** Single-variable Differential & Integral Calculus (Lectures: 15)  
  Focuses on limits, continuity, differentiability, Mean Value Theorems, Taylor's theorem, Riemann integrals, improper integrals.

- **Weeks 10–13:** Multivariable Differential and Integral Calculus (Lectures: 12)  
  Topics include functions of several variables, partial derivatives, directional derivatives, maxima/minima, multiple integrals.

**Assessment Strategy:**

- **Quiz 1:** 10% (Weightage: 10%) - Covers foundational concepts from Weeks 1–3.
- **Midsem Exam:** 20% (Weightage: 20%) - Focuses on material from Weeks 1–9.
- **Endsem Examination:** 45% (Weightage: 45%) - Comprehensive assessment of the entire syllabus, emphasizing deeper conceptual understanding and application.
- **Homeworks:** Best 10 out of 12 assignments contribute to 25% of the final grade.

1. **Textbook:** G. B. Thomas and R. L. Finney, Calculus, Pearson  
   Offers comprehensive coverage from single-variable to multivariable calculus.
   
2. **Reference Books:**  
   - R. Goldberg, Methods of Real Analysis, Oxford & Ibh  
   - K. A. Ross, Elementary Analysis: The Theory of Calculus, Springer  
   - Walter Rudin, Principles of Mathematical Analysis, McGraw-Hill  
   - N. Piskunov, Differential and Integral Calculus, Volume I, CBS

These resources provide additional depth and alternative explanations for complex topics.

**Conclusion:**  
This course is structured to build a strong foundation in real analysis and calculus, preparing students for advanced studies in mathematics, physics, engineering, and related fields. The combination of theoretical instruction and practical application ensures a well-rounded learning experience.
