### Course Overview  
**Course Code:** SOC 212  
**Course Name:** Key Concepts in Economic Sociology  
**Credits:** 4  
**Offered To:** Undergraduate Students (UG)  

---

### Course Description  
This course provides an in-depth exploration of the principles and theories central to economic sociology. It examines how human economic actions are influenced by societal structures, group dynamics, legal institutions, power imbalances, organizations, and cultural norms. The course encourages students to critically analyze contemporary issues by integrating insights from economics and sociology. Key topics include Karl Polanyi's formalism and substantive approaches, embeddedness theory by Granovetter, Swedberg's contributions on economic life, and the sociological understanding of exchange forms (gifts, reciprocity, and market dynamics). Students will engage with primary texts such as *Economies and Cultures* by Richard Wilk, *Economic Anthropology* by Chris Hann and Keith Hart, and works by Durkheim, Granovetter, and Swedberg.

### Course Outcomes (COs)  
By the end of this course, students will be able to:  
- **CO1:** Demonstrate a comprehensive understanding of key concepts in economic sociology by reading and comprehending primary texts.  
- **CO2:** Analyze cross-disciplinary connections between economics and society, identifying how economic actions are embedded within social structures.  
- **CO3:** Understand and explain various methodological approaches in mainstream economics and sociology to critically evaluate contemporary issues.

### Assessment Plan  
The course assessment is designed to evaluate students' understanding of key concepts, analytical skills, and ability to synthesize course material.  

- **Close reading, presentation, and analysis of key text:** 20% (Evaluates CO1 and CO2)  
- **Quiz:** 20% (Evaluates CO1 and CO2)  
- **Assignment:** 10% (Evaluates CO3)  
- **End-Semester Examination:** 50% (Evaluates CO1, CO2, CO3, CO4)
