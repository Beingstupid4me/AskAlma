### Course Overview  
**Course Name:** Macroeconomics  
**Course Code:** ECO201  
**Credits:** 4  
**Offered To:** Undergraduate Students (UG)  

This course provides an introduction to the fundamental models of macroeconomics, focusing on analyzing aggregate behavior within economies. It covers key topics such as macroeconomic models, demand and supply analysis, fiscal and monetary policy, inflation, unemployment, and economic growth. The course is designed to equip students with the ability to understand and evaluate macroeconomic policies and their impacts on various sectors of the economy.

---

### Prerequisites  
- **Mandatory:** None specified.  
- **Desirable:** A basic understanding of microeconomics and economics principles is recommended but not required.  
- **Other:** None specified.  

Students are expected to have a foundational knowledge of economic concepts, though this course will build upon those basics.

---

### Learning Outcomes (COs)  
By the end of this course, students will be able to:  

1. **CO1:** Explain basic macroeconomic models and their application in analyzing aggregate behavior using supply and demand frameworks.  
2. **CO2:** Understand key macroeconomic variables, including inflation, unemployment, and income distribution, and calculate them effectively.  
3. **CO3:** Analyze medium- and long-run price adjustments and their impact on equilibrium income and other macroeconomic factors.  
4. **CO4:** Examine the role of money in the economy and evaluate the effects of monetary policy changes on economic growth, inflation, and employment.  
5. **CO5:** Assess the impact of macroeconomic shocks, such as fiscal policy actions, on inflation and unemployment, while also understanding the role of government debt and deficits.

### Assessment Strategy  

The course assessment is designed to evaluate both theoretical understanding and practical application of macroeconomic concepts. The breakdown is as follows:  

- **Homework Assignments (5):** 20% Contribution  
  - Includes weekly problem sets, analysis of economic data, and case studies.  
  - Late submissions will incur a penalty of up to 10%.  

- **Writing Assignments (2):** 20% Contribution  
  - Two essays on key macroeconomic topics, such as the impact of fiscal policy or the role of monetary policy in managing inflation.  
  - Students are encouraged to use real-world examples and data for their analyses.  

- **Mid-Semester Exam (2):** 30% Contribution  
  - Covers material from Weeks 1–9.  
  - The exam will include both multiple-choice questions and short-answer problems.  

- **Final Exam:** 30% Contribution  
  - Comprehensive exam covering all course material, including case studies and policy analysis.  
  - Students are advised to review all lecture notes and assignments before the final exam.
