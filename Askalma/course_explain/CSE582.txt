**Course Overview**

The course CSE582, titled "Software Production, Evolution, and Maintenance (SPEM)" is a 4-credit offering for both Postgraduate (PG) and Undergraduate (UG) students. It focuses on the development, evolution, maintenance, and preservation of large-scale software products using the Software Product Line (SPL) paradigm. The course also emphasizes continuous improvement through software evolution and maintenance processes.

**Prerequisites**

Students enrolling in this course should have a basic understanding of software engineering concepts and techniques. This foundational knowledge is essential for grasping the advanced topics covered in the course, such as product line engineering and maintenance practices.

**Learning Outcomes (COs)**

By the end of this course, students will be able to:

- **CO1:** Develop the framework of product line engineering for large-scale software production.
- **CO2:** Implement software maintenance processes and techniques during software development.
- **CO3:** Distinguish between existing software system artifacts and develop functionality and quality attributes through re-engineering.
- **CO4:** Construct common and variable domain-specific requirements by analyzing challenges in domain-specific requirement engineering.

**Weekly Lecture Plan**

The course is structured over 13 weeks, with each week focusing on specific topics that align with the learning outcomes:

- **Week 1:** Introduction to Software Evolution vs. Maintenance
- **Week 2:** Overview of Software Evolution and Maintenance Models
- **Week 3:** Software Product Line Engineering - A Motivation
- **Week 4:** Software Framework and Variability
- **Week 5:** Orthogonal Variability Model
- **Week 6:** Domain-Specific Requirements Engineering
- **Week 7:** Software Re-engineering
- **Week 8:** Continued focus on Software Re-engineering
- **Week 9:** Introduction to DevOps and Containers
- **Week 10:** Microservice Architecture
- **Week 11:** Continued exploration of Microservice Architecture
- **Week 12:** Legacy Information Systems
- **Week 13:** Student Presentations and Project Report Discussions

Each week includes assignments, quizzes, or presentations to reinforce learning outcomes.

**Assessment Strategy**

The course employs a comprehensive assessment strategy to evaluate student understanding and mastery:

- **Homework Exercises:** 20% of the final grade, focusing on practical application of concepts.
- **Weekly Quizzes:** 10% of the final grade, assessing immediate comprehension of weekly topics.
- **Mid-Semester Examination:** 20% of the final grade, evaluating cumulative understanding up to mid-term.
- **End-Semester Examination:** 20% of the final grade, covering the entire syllabus.
- **Term Project/Paper Report:** 20% of the final grade, emphasizing practical project work and research.

This assessment structure ensures a balanced evaluation of both theoretical knowledge and practical skills.
