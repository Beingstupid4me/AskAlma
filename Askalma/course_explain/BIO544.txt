**Course Overview: Computational Gastronomy**

**Course Code:** BIO544  
**Course Name:** Computational Gastronomy  
**Credits:** 4  
**Offered To:** Undergraduate (UG) and Postgraduate (PG) students  

This course is designed to introduce students to the emerging field of Computational Gastronomy, which integrates food science with data-driven techniques. The primary objectives include understanding the science of food, analyzing culinary data using statistical methods, machine learning, and computational tools, and exploring opportunities in food-tech industries, entrepreneurship, policy-making, and sustainable development.

**Prerequisites:**  
- A strong command in programming (Python) and data analysis.  
- Exposure to statistical analysis, data visualization, machine learning, and natural language processing is highly recommended.  

**Course Outcomes (COs):**  
1. **CO1:** Students will be able to explain the basic tenets of the science of food and its scope for data-driven analysis.  
2. **CO2:** Students will learn to analyze and interpret major aspects of culinary data, applying statistical methods and visualization techniques.  
3. **CO3:** Students will develop the ability to design classification and prediction models from culinary data using machine learning tools.

**Weekly Lecture Plan:**  
The course is structured over 14 weeks, with each week focusing on specific topics that build upon one another:  
- Weeks 1 & 2: Introduction to the science of food, including its history, cultural influences, role of technology, and the integration of data science into culinary practices.  
- Weeks 3 & 4: Culinary data analysis from a recipe perspective, covering data structures, compilation, curation, visualization, and global trends in cuisine.  
- Weeks 5 & 6: Analysis of flavor data, exploring sensory attributes and molecular composition to understand food pairing.  
- Weeks 7 & 8: Culinary data analysis focusing on health and nutrition, including data visualization techniques.  
- Week 9: Modeling and prediction in cuisine classification, utilizing machine learning algorithms.  
- Weeks 10 & 11: Prediction of taste and odor using machine learning models, with a focus on transforming recipes across cuisines.  
- Weeks 12 & 13: Opportunities and challenges in data-driven culinary science, including food-tech innovations and sustainable practices.  

**Weekly Lab Plan:**  
The laboratory exercises complement the lectures by providing hands-on experience with data analysis tools and machine learning models. Each week includes practical tasks aimed at reinforcing theoretical concepts.

**Assessment Plan:**  
- **Mid-Semester Exam (15%):** Evaluates foundational knowledge of culinary data analysis.  
- **Assignments (30%):** Includes a mix of reading materials, exercises, and projects across the course duration.  
- **Mini-Project (20%):** A comprehensive project involving data analysis and modeling in a culinary context.  
- **End-Semester Exam (35%):** Assesses overall understanding and application of concepts learned throughout the course.

**Resource Materials:**  
The course utilizes a combination of textbooks and research articles to provide a well-rounded learning experience:  
- Textbooks include "Cooked: A Natural History of Transformation" by Michael Pollan.  
- Research articles cover topics such as flavor networks, culinary data analysis in Indian cuisine, machine learning applications in food science, and the benefits of computational approaches in gastronomy.

This structured approach ensures that students gain a comprehensive understanding of Computational Gastronomy, equipping them with the skills to analyze and innovate in this interdisciplinary field.
