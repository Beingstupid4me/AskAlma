### Course Overview  
**Course Code:** ECE539  
**Course Name:** Wireless System Implementation  
**Credits:** 4  
**Offered To:** Undergraduate/Postgraduate (UG / PG)  

This course is designed to provide students with a comprehensive understanding of wireless communication systems. It combines theoretical concepts with practical implementation, focusing on the design and analysis of wireless systems. Students will explore topics such as large-scale propagation models, wireless channel characterization, digital modulation techniques, and advanced wireless technologies like OFDM. The course also includes hands-on projects using tools such as NI USRP and MATLAB to reinforce theoretical knowledge.

---

### Course Description  
This course equips students with the ability to analyze and design basic wireless communication systems. Starting with large-scale propagation models, students will study the modeling of wireless channels as linear time-varying filters, statistical multipath models, and wireless digital modulation/demodulation techniques. The performance of these techniques in Rayleigh fading channels and spatial diversity systems will be evaluated. Additionally, the course delves into the trade-offs between single-carrier and multicarrier (e.g., OFDM) modulation techniques. A hardware-software project using NI USRP or MATLAB is included to validate theoretical concepts.

### Course Outcomes (COs)  
By the end of this course, students will be able to:  
1. **CO1:** Analyze the behavior of large-scale and small-scale wireless propagation models.  
2. **CO2:** Evaluate the performance of wireless communication systems in various channel conditions using digital modulation techniques such as BPSK, QPSK, and OFDM.  
3. **CO3:** Understand and implement basic wireless communication systems using software tools like MATLAB or GNU Radio.  
4. **CO4:** Design and optimize wireless communication systems for different applications (e.g., LOS vs NLOS links).

### Assessment Strategy  
The course assessment is divided as follows:  
- **Assignments:** 30% (Includes MATLAB/Simulink experiments, reports, and quizzes.)  
- **Final Project:** 30% (Comprehensive project involving system design, simulation, and presentation.)  
- **Quizzes and Short Tests:** 20% (Weekly quizzes to assess understanding of key concepts.)  
- **End-Semester Examination:** 20% (Comprehensive exam covering all course topics with a focus on practical applications.)  

The end-semester examination carries a significant weight, reflecting its importance in evaluating overall understanding.
