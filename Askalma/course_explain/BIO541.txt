**Course Overview**

The course titled "Data Sciences in Genomics" is designed to equip students with advanced computational skills necessary for analyzing genomic data. This field is pivotal as it integrates high-throughput technologies with genomics to address complex biological questions, driving genomic projects forward.

**Course Details**

- **Course Code**: BIO541
- **Course Name**: Data Sciences in Genomics
- **Credits**: 4
- **Offered To**: Undergraduate and Postgraduate students (UG/PG)
- **Course Description**: This course delves into the application of computational methods to genomic data, covering foundational concepts, advanced techniques, and practical applications. It aims to provide students with a comprehensive understanding of genomic data analysis, from initial processing to meaningful insights.

**Prerequisites**

- **Mandatory Prerequisites**: None specified.
- **Desirable Prerequisites**: Basic knowledge in molecular biology and familiarity with computational tools is recommended.
- **Other Prerequisites**: None specified.

**Learning Outcomes (COs)**

1. **CO1**: Students will understand the source, properties, and significance of genomic data.
2. **CO2**: Students will master tools for processing various genomic data types.
3. **CO3**: Students will comprehend statistical approaches used in genomic data analysis.
4. **CO4**: Students will perform complex post-processing tasks to derive meaningful biological insights.

**Weekly Lecture Plan**

- **Week 1**: Introduction to molecular cell biology, central dogma, and measurement techniques using microarrays and NGS technologies.
- **Week 2-3**: Basics of genetics and their clinical applications.
- **Week 4**: Exploration of NGS concepts, DNA alignment algorithms, data QC, and filtering.
- **Week 5**: Applications in single-cell studies, addressing biases like dropouts and noise.
- **Week 6-7**: Unsupervised methods (clustering and biclustering) and supervised approaches (classification), along with normalization techniques for microarray, bulk RNA-seq, and single-cell RNA-seq data.
- **Week 8**: Network inference methods, including WGCNA and visualization techniques.
- **Week 9-10**: Epigenome analysis, noise handling, and application of epigenome data.
- **Week 11**: Assembly processes, assessment, refinement, and variant calling (SNPs, MNPs, small indels).
- **Week 12-13**: Variant annotations, interpretation methods, statistical prediction, QTL analysis, and data visualization techniques for population genomics.
- **Project Presentations**: Students present their final projects.

**Assessment Strategy**

- **Mid-Semester Examination**: Contributes 20% to the final grade.
- **End-Semester Examination**: Contributes another 20%.
- **Assignments**: Regular tasks contribute 20% in total (approximately 5 assignments).
- **Project**: The project accounts for 40% of the grade, emphasizing practical application.

**Learning Resources**

- **Textbooks**: 
  - "Genetics: A Conceptual Approach" by B. Pierce
  - Online resources from Next Generation Sequencing (NGS) projects.
  - "Next-Generation Sequencing Data Analysis" by Xinkun Wang
- **Publications**: Articles on ChIP-seq analysis, RNA-Seq reviews, and field guides to whole-genome sequencing.

**Course Outcomes**

Students will be able to:
- Understand genomic data sources and their properties.
- Utilize tools for processing diverse genomic data types.
- Apply statistical methods in genomic data analysis.
- Perform complex post-processing tasks to derive meaningful biological insights.

This structured approach ensures a comprehensive understanding of genomic data analysis, preparing students with the necessary skills for advanced research and applications in computational genomics.
