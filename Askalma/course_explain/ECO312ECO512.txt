### Course Overview  
**Course Code:** ECO312/ECO512  
**Course Name:** Industrial Organization (IO)  
**Credits:** 4  
**Offered To:** Undergraduate (UG) and Postgraduate (PG) students  
**Course Description:** This course provides an in-depth introduction to the theoretical foundations of industrial organization. It explores how firms operate in competitive markets, focusing on strategic decision-making under market power. Key topics include profit maximization, advertising, product differentiation, pricing strategies, and market entry/exit dynamics. The course also integrates empirical methods to analyze real-world scenarios, offering students a comprehensive understanding of firm behavior in complex market structures.

### Prerequisites  
- **Mandatory:** ECO101 or ECO311 (Microeconomics)  
- **Desirable:** Completion of Game Theory (ECO425/ECO625)  
- **Other:** None specified  

---


### Learning Outcomes (Course Outcomes)  
By the end of this course, students will be able to:  
- **CO1**: Understand how to approach problems in IO by analyzing strategic interactions between firms.  
- **CO2**: Model decisions that influence profit, such as advertising, product differentiation, and collusion.  
- **CO3**: Analyze decision-making processes in monopolies, duopolies, and vertically controlled markets.  
- **CO4**: Justify the motivations behind a firm's entry into or exit from a market.  
- **CO5**: Implement basic empirical and applied theory techniques in IO, particularly for advanced-level courses (5XX).

### Assessment Strategy  
The course assessment is designed to evaluate both theoretical understanding and practical application of concepts. The breakdown is as follows:  

| Type of Evaluation       | % Contribution in Grade (3XX) | % Contribution in Grade (5XX) |
|---------------------------|-------------------------------|-------------------------------|
| Homework Assignments      | 30%                           | 20%                           |
| Mid-Semester Examination   | 30%                           | 30%                           |
| Final Examination         | 40%                           | 30%                           |
| Project                    | 20%                           | 20%                           |

The mid-semester and final examinations carry significant weightage because they assess students' ability to synthesize and apply course concepts in complex scenarios.

### Resource Materials  
The primary textbook for this course is **"Industrial Organization: Markets and Strategies"** by Belleflamme, Paul, and Martin Peitz (2015). This book provides a comprehensive introduction to the field. For advanced-level courses (5XX), students are recommended to use **"The Theory of Industrial Organization"** by Jean Tirole (Eastern Economy Edition). Additional scholarly papers will be provided for 5XX students as part of their learning materials.

This structured approach ensures that students gain a thorough understanding of industrial organization, from foundational theories to practical applications.
