### Course Overview  
**Course Name:** Stochastic Estimation and Control  
**Course Code:** ECE672  
**Credits:** 2  
**Offered To:** Undergraduate (UG) and Postgraduate (PG) students  

This course provides an in-depth understanding of stochastic estimation techniques and their applications in control systems. The curriculum begins with foundational concepts in probability, random variables, and stochastic processes, progressing to advanced topics such as the Kalman filter for linear systems and particle filters for nonlinear systems. Students will also explore practical applications of these methods in dynamic systems.

---

### Prerequisites  
**Mandatory Pre-requisites:**  
- **Probability and Random Processes (ECE501)**  

**Desirable Pre-requisites:** None specified.  

**Other Pre-requisites:** None applicable.  

Students are expected to have a solid foundation in basic probability theory and linear algebra before enrolling in this course.

---

### Course Outcomes (COs)  
By the end of this course, students will be able to:  
- **CO1:** Develop and linearize a measurement model for a dynamical system.  
- **CO2:** Select an appropriate state estimator based on the system's properties, such as nonlinearities or noise characteristics.  
- **CO3:** Design a linear or nonlinear state estimator for systems with process and measurement noise.  
- **CO4:** Apply estimation techniques to real-world problems involving dynamical systems.  
- **CO5:** Evaluate the performance of estimators and interpret their results in practical scenarios.

### Weekly Lecture Plan  

| Week Number | Lecture Topic | Assignments/Labs/Tutorials |
|-------------|---------------|---------------------------|
| 1           | Introduction to probability and random processes: random variables, PDF, total probability theorem, Bayes’ Theorem, stochastic processes, stationarity, ergodicity | Homework 1 |
| 2           | Basics of estimation: Parameter estimation, maximum likelihood estimation, least squares estimation, maximum a posteriori estimation, minimum mean square estimation | Homework 1 |
| 3           | Estimation of dynamic systems: Specialize to LTI systems, state space form, Recursive static estimation, minimum variance estimator | Homework 2 |
| 4           | The discrete time Kalman filter | Homework 2 |
| 5           | The continuous time Kalman filter (f) | Homework 3 |
| 6           | Nonlinearity in dynamic systems, measurement models/likelihood functions, linearization | Homework 3 |
| 7           | Nonlinear estimators: Extended Kalman filter, sampling a PDF, particle filter | Homework 4 |

### Assessment Plan  
The course assessment is divided as follows:  

- **Homework:** 60% (Regular assignments to reinforce concepts)  
- **Final Exam:** 40% (Comprehensive evaluation of the entire course content)  

Both homework and exams carry equal weightage, ensuring a balanced assessment of students' understanding.
