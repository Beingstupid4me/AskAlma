**Credits:** 4

---

### **Course Description**
This course provides a comprehensive exploration of behavioural economics, blending theoretical concepts with practical applications. It begins by introducing the foundational principles of behaviourism and cognitive science to explain human decision-making processes that deviate from traditional rationality assumptions. The curriculum is divided into weekly modules, each focusing on specific topics such as decision-making under certainty, uncertainty, risk theory, bounded rationality, intertemporal choice, and strategic interactions. These concepts are applied through laboratory experiments designed to assess and enhance students' understanding of human behaviour in economic contexts.

The course also delves into advanced topics like altruism, social preferences, and the impact of strategic settings on decision-making. Students will engage with primary literature and textbooks to critically analyze these phenomena. The assessment plan includes summarizing key papers, designing lab experiments, a mid-semester examination, and a final exam, ensuring a well-rounded evaluation of both theoretical knowledge and practical skills.

---

### **Prerequisites**
- **Mandatory:** MTH201 Probability and Statistics  
- **Desirable:** Microeconomics  

---

### **Learning Outcomes (COs)**
By the end of this course, students will be able to:  
1. Identify how individuals influenced by behavioural biases behave differently from perfectly rational agents.  
2. Explain the relevance of risk, uncertainty, and time in individual decisions.  
3. Determine the role of social preferences and strategic settings in decision-making.  
4. Implement theories to analyse experimental economics, policy choices, firm decisions, and financial market dynamics.  
5. Design lab experiments to identify and estimate behavioural biases.
