### Course Overview: Introduction to Quantum Computing

This course is designed as an introductory module in quantum computing, offering students a foundational understanding of the principles, models, and algorithms that underpin this transformative field. The course is structured to cater to both computer science undergraduates and postgraduates, providing a blend of theoretical knowledge and practical applications.

#### Course Code: CSE622
- **Course Name:** Introduction to Quantum Computing  
- **Credits:** 4  
- **Offered To:** UG+PG (Undergraduate + Postgraduate) students  

The course is delivered over a comprehensive period, with weekly lectures and hands-on lab sessions. Students are expected to engage deeply with the material through assignments, quizzes, and practical exercises.

---

### Course Description

This course introduces students to the fundamental concepts of quantum computing, emphasizing its potential to revolutionize computational problem-solving. While certain problems can be solved more efficiently using quantum models compared to classical platforms (like digital circuits or Turing machines), other scenarios may yield results comparable to or even inferior to classical approaches. The curriculum is divided into two main halves:

1. **First Half:**  
   - Introduction to the postulates of quantum computing, including operations and operators.
   - Study of basic structures in quantum computing models, such as circuits and algorithms.
   - Exploration of intriguing solutions like quantum teleportation, super-dense coding, and the Deutsch-Jozsa algorithm.

2. **Second Half:**  
   - Advanced topics include quantum Fourier transformation, amplitude amplification, and eigenvalue estimation.
   - Examination of key quantum algorithms like Grover’s search, Shor's factoring, and BB84 protocols.
   - Discussion of recent advancements in quantum computing research.

Students are encouraged to read recent or classical research papers and simulate algorithms using quantum circuit simulators (e.g., Microsoft LIQUi) to gain practical insights into the system.

---

### Prerequisites

To successfully engage with this course, students should have a solid foundation in the following areas:

- **Mandatory:**  
  - **MTH100 Linear Algebra:** Essential for understanding the mathematical underpinnings of quantum computing.
  - **Probability and Statistics:** Required for analyzing quantum algorithms and their outcomes.

- **Desirable:**  
  - **Analysis and Design of Algorithms:** A strong background in classical algorithm design will be beneficial.

Students are encouraged to consult with the course instructor if they have additional prerequisites or concerns regarding their preparedness for the course.

---

### Course Outcomes (COs)

By the end of this course, students will be able to:

- **CO1:** Understand the principles of quantum computing and its applications in various domains.  
- **CO2:** Analyze and design quantum algorithms and circuits using different models.  
- **CO3:** Implement simple quantum algorithms and explain their functionality through written homeworks and simulator-based exercises.  
- **CO4:** Present research papers, conduct paper readings, and apply advanced concepts to real-world problems.
